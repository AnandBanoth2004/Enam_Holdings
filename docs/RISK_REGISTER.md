# FINN Risk Register

Prototype risks are listed with practical mitigations. The design should prefer visible uncertainty and useful abstention over fabricated certainty.

| ID | Risk | Why it matters | Practical mitigation | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R-01 | External data access instability | Free endpoints may change, rate-limit, block automated access, or return incomplete/stale data. | Use adapters per source, cache responses with fetch timestamps, set conservative retries/timeouts, surface stale/unavailable status, and provide curated fixtures for reliable demo fallback without presenting them as live. | High |
| R-02 | NSE/BSE scraping limitations | Exchange pages/endpoints may require cookies, change markup, throttle requests, or restrict automated access; data may be delayed or incomplete. | Prefer documented/legitimate access and downloadable filings; use low-rate scheduled fetches, cache, monitor failures, retain primary filing URLs, and avoid bypassing access controls. Use a labeled curated filing set if live access is unreliable. | High |
| R-03 | Financial PDF extraction errors | Tables may be multi-column, scanned, rotated, footnoted, or use inconsistent labels/units; OCR can corrupt digits/signs. | Keep document/page provenance; detect text vs scan; validate extracted values against table headers, periods, units, and totals; use human-reviewed fixtures for the demo; show low-confidence values as unverified/omitted. | High |
| R-04 | LLM hallucination or unsupported synthesis | A plausible but false financial figure, quote, or explanation can mislead investment judgment. | Restrict Pull context to primary documents; require citations/excerpts; separate reported facts, calculations, and interpretation; deterministic formulas; ask for abstention when evidence is absent; test adversarial/unanswerable prompts. | Critical |
| R-05 | Missing financial periods | Companies may omit, restate, or report only some periods; annual reports and quarterly filings may use different presentations. | Build a period-availability check; compare only compatible periods; never interpolate silently; display “not available” and identify exactly which period is missing. | High |
| R-06 | Standalone/consolidated or units mismatch | Similar-looking values may have different reporting scope, units, fiscal periods, or accounting definitions. | Store/display reporting basis, currency, units, and period with every value; reject mismatched comparisons; convert units only through explicit, tested transformations. | High |
| R-07 | Entity matching errors | Common names, initials, misspellings, director names, and company aliases can connect a filing to the wrong company/person/contact. | Resolve companies using exchange/security identifiers and aliases; match people with multiple corroborating attributes (employer, role, dates, education); record confidence/evidence; require review/confirmation for ambiguous matches and never claim a relationship from name alone. | High |
| R-08 | Network privacy and consent | Contacts' employment and education data are personal information; accidental exposure or unauthorized collection undermines trust. | Use synthetic profiles in the prototype and label them; in production use consented/authorized sources, tenant isolation, least privilege, encryption, retention/deletion controls, audit trails, and explicit access/consent policy. | Critical |
| R-09 | Professional-platform terms/access | Automated profile scraping may violate platform terms or be technically blocked. | Do not scrape restricted profiles; use data the user can legitimately access, licensed/public datasets, or synthetic demo data. Document the real-world acquisition path and permissions. | High |
| R-10 | Network-derived inference overclaim | Shared school/employer or second-degree path does not prove a close relationship, expertise, or willingness to advise. | Show the underlying link and dates; label inferred relevance as a lead, not endorsement; let user confirm relationship strength; avoid sensitive or unsupported personal inferences. | High |
| R-11 | Network sent to an external model | Prompts can expose names, contact graphs, or career data to model providers. | Minimize and pseudonymize context; keep sensitive matching deterministic/local when feasible; configure provider data handling; do not send real contacts in the demo; disclose the production data flow. | High |
| R-12 | Market-data and alert timing | EOD data is not real-time; source update times, holidays, split adjustments, and stale values can cause false/missed alerts. | Label EOD timing and timezone; track source timestamp and market date; define the volume averaging window and adjusted/unadjusted price policy; suppress alerts with stale/missing baselines. | Medium |
| R-13 | Announcement taxonomy ambiguity | A filing may fit multiple labels or none; a classification error can hide a material item. | Treat the 17 labels as a practical default, allow multi-label/uncertain results, preserve the original filing, and route low confidence to “unclassified” rather than suppressing it. | High |
| R-14 | API/model costs and rate limits | Repeated document ingestion or large prompts can exceed free tiers or slow the demo. | Cache documents and derived analyses, chunk only relevant pages, use deterministic rules/cheap models for bulk tasks, route difficult synthesis selectively, enforce budgets/rate limits, and show graceful delayed/error states. | Medium |
| R-15 | Deployment constraints / demo fragility | A working local app but unavailable hosted service, missing secrets, or cold-start failure makes evaluation impossible. | Choose a simple deployable web stack; keep build reproducible; use environment variables and no committed secrets; provide seed data and a read-only demo path; smoke-test the public link and phone layout before submission. | High |
| R-16 | Thin prototype appears broader than it is | Mock data or partial workflows can be mistaken for live/complete coverage. | Label synthetic, cached, delayed, or illustrative records in the UI; state coverage limits; keep demo flows narrow and fully working; list deliberate omissions in the covering note. | High |
| R-17 | Financial calculations mislead on negative/zero bases | Percentage changes can be meaningless when a comparison-period profit is zero/negative or changes sign. | Show absolute change, avoid or qualify percentage growth where mathematically misleading, and test zero/negative transitions. | Medium |
| R-18 | Broken primary-source links or document changes | A source may move or a transcript may be unavailable, undermining citations. | Store source title, issuer, period, fetch time, and URL; retain permitted cached metadata/excerpts; detect broken links; clearly distinguish unavailable evidence from model inference. | Medium |

## Key controls to show in the prototype

- Visible source/date labels and links for every filing and research claim.
- Per-field period, unit, and standalone/consolidated basis for financial comparisons.
- “Unavailable” / “needs verification” states rather than fabricated values.
- Synthetic-network labeling and no real contact data in the demonstration.
- Clear EOD and data-freshness labels; thresholds and averaging window shown to the investor.
- A visible distinction between filing fact, calculated metric, and AI-generated interpretation.
